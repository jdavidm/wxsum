## Summary

**Errors that a reader or the editor would notice**

1. **Article 5.4 (L737-748).** The listing does not come from its command. The command lists `year >= 2016`, and the do-file and log show 2016-2023 (8 rows). The article shows only 2019-2023, and the text says "the last five windows". The SJ asks for every output in the article to come from the log. You can fix this in one of two ways:
   - change the command to `year >= 2019` everywhere: the do-file, then rerun the log and `audit/sync_docs.py` (help, README, index.html);
   - or restore the rows for 2016-2018 and the separator line.
2. **Article 5.4 (L751).** "(1053 minus 2761.6)" should be "(1053 minus 271.6)".
3. **Article 5.1 (L598).** "and saves the resulting dataset": the command saves nothing (example 7 is the one with `save()`).
4. **Article section 2 (L19).** It still does not mention `set maxvar`, although editor item 9 asked for exactly that in the article's discussion of capacity. The response points to the help file instead.
5. **References.** Two entries render wrongly:
   - ERA5 prints "A. Schepers, D.and Simmons" (a missing space in the .bib file);
   - Dinku (2020) prints "Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Climate Science 30 July."
6. **Response to the editor (L27).** "much more efficient in ... the number of lines in the code" is not true. `wxsum.ado` grew from 905 lines in version 5 to 1,617, plus 690 in `wxprep.ado`. The claim about speed holds.
7. **Help, README, readme.txt, and do-file say the examples are those "of the article".** The article shows 8 of the 12, and `wxprep.sthlp` L189 says "examples 10-12 of the article", but example 12 is not in it.
8. **`wxsum.sthlp` L8** has a jump link to "Changes from version 5", a section that no longer exists.
9. **`sj paper/wxsum_submission.pdf` is out of date.** It has 28 pages and was built on 22 October, before section 5 and your penultimate edits. The current source builds to 34 pages. `main.tex` was deleted in `afeeb1a`, so the PDF cannot be rebuilt from the repository as it stands.
10. **`wxsum.pkg` and `stata.toc` start with `v 6.0`.** The `v` line is the version of the file format and should read `v 3` ([R] net). This is corrected in the version 7.0 commit (section 5.4).

## 7. Checked and found consistent

- **Options, defaults, and limits:** the same in the article, `wxsum.sthlp`, the README, and `wxsum.ado`:
  - `lr_years()` 2-50 with a default of 10; `ref_min()` at least 2, default all; `minobs(1)`; `rain_threshold(1)`;
  - `tmp_bin()` 1-42; at most 41 `cuts()`; at most 100 GDD categories; stubs of at most 24 characters in `wxprep`;
  - `shape(long)` by default; event windows from `event_date - days - lag` to `event_date - 1 - lag`;
  - 29 February becomes 28 February in common years.
- **Syntax:** the diagrams of both commands match their `syntax` statements, including `generate()` abbreviated to `gen()`.
- **Stored results:**
  - Tables 3-6 of the article, the help files, and the code (`return list` in example 2 of the log) list the same results;
  - the columns of `r(missing)` are as documented (shares for `any_missing` and `all_missing`, counts for `below_minobs`). For unit-specific windows, `days` is the mean window length, a detail the article does not mention.
- **Output in the article:** every output block except the one in 5.4 is in the log, and the do-file reproduces the log (the docs and install suites run it from a `net get` folder).
- **Example data and the article agree:**
  - simulated yields with coefficients 1.0, 0.25, and -15, and birth weights with -4 g and -9 g per heat day (the `notes` of `hh_panel.dta` and `births.dta`);
  - 300 births from 1 January 2014 to 29 November 2023; 10 households from 2013 through 2023; 87,600 hourly readings of 2022.
- **Spelling:** Word finds no misspelled prose words apart from "Github" and the subdaily and sub-daily pair. The other words it flagged are command, option, and variable names.
- **Formulas of section 4:** checked numerically against `wxsum` on 21 October (54,610 values). Your rewrite of 22 October (`57d7b65`) restates several of them: the median in words, the temperature bins as cases, and the monthly mean without the identity total / months. This review checked every current formula against the code and the help by hand, not numerically, and they agree:
  - the bin edges $\ell + (j - 2)w$ to $\ell + (j - 1)w$ end at $h$ for $j = J - 1$, as in the code since 6.3.1;
  - `gddcat` is $\beta + j$ for the regular intervals;
  - the spell examples give 1/2/0 and 2/3/3.
