Dear Jeffrey and colleagues

Thanks for your submission to the SJ. We've heard back from an expert reviewer – please see the file attached. You’ll see that the referee is broadly supportive, but has some “concerns and recommendations” for you to address.

I have looked at your materials too and have the following comments (not necessarily in order of importance):

1.	I support the referee’s general remarks, and also the requests for greater functionality. 

2.	To me, you’ve provided useful functionality but it’s fairly narrow in terms of constraints on what is required in for inputs and the nature of the outputs. A devil’s advocate might argue that -wxsum- in its current form is really helpful for your group and those like you, but not for a wider SJ community of users of climate/weather data. Naturally, the SJ has the interests of the latter at heart. So, I think you take a two-pronged strategy in revisions: (i) make a stronger case that the current functionality is actually of widespread utility (i.e., even if the focus is narrow, there are lots of researchers who could use the functionality); (ii) help the wider Stata-using community more.

3.	Relatedly, the nature of the variables used and provided seem rather field-specific, specifically, ag and ag econ focused. I am more familiar with the use of climate/weather data in the biosocial field (including child development). See, e.g., the discussion of a wide(r) variety of heat metrics in the the Plos-One article at https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0330498 (disclosure: lead author is one of my PhD students, now graduated). (Compared to your variables, there are metrics related to e.g. wet bulb temperature, and humidity, etc.)  This raises the question of how to deal with variables summarising metrics at the input stage that (a) differ from your example sets (to what extent are they hard-wired?), and possibly (b) combine heat and rainfall data to produce ‘composite’ metrics?  Also, the nature of a “season” sounds ag-specific. Could one, for instance, use other period definitions such as months of pregnancy length (start and end dates are not tied to calendar time). Is there a way for users to produce differently-defined output variables (everything seems quite hard-wired at present)? 

4.	Relatedly, the referee’s remarks are pertinent here, including provision of a companion command to help prepare data for the tightly-constrained input format you require (both in terms of wide format, format of variable names, etc.)

5.	Relatedly, I think you need to add functionality (“post-calculation” commands) or otherwise provide examples so that users can find out and check the nature of the datasets created by -wxsum-. Things that come to my mind from playing with your code include: the number of variables created, the number of separate units identified by some ID variable, patterns of missing, and also save for the record some or all of other choices such as start and end dates. If you made your command r(class), then it would be useful to save some of this stuff in r() results that could be later retrieved (cf. -return list-). You could also have the program pump out some of the stuff to the output window once the command has finished.

6.	Relatedly, you could do more to show your command in action. Your Section 5 “examples” in the text are problematic, not least because you don’t show any output or indeed post-calculation use of the created data. I’m not saying you need to provide a full-blown “mini article” for illustrative purposes; what I’m saying is that the examples need extending in a way that better demonstrates the dataset in action in research. You write about merging with other data for analysis. How about finding a small ‘toy’ dataset you can share, and do some illustrative analysis?  

7.	Relatedly, I would have thought that long form output is the most convenient format for Stata users if they want to inspect the created variables prior to a merge (… and maybe if the to-be-merged data are long as well). In my playing around, I ended up going long to “look at” the rain data because wide was simply far too unwieldy. 

8.	The term “killing degree” is used a lot but the label – a strange one! – is not explained

9.	In your discussion of Stata capacity, you need to explicitly discuss -set maxvar-. (Note how when I used your rain dta set, Stata complained until I reset maxvar.)

10.	A related problem: you don’t provide any example do-files and the (plain text) log-files generated by them (cf. mine attached). We must have there, at the very least producing everything that you paste into the article text. See the Submission Guidelines. 

11.	Conclusions: this is simply a summary, not providing genuine conclusions, hence risking redundant repetition. We want some genuine forward-looking conclusions. See the discussion of this Section in the Submission Guidelines to understand what we want, and rewrite the section accordingly (with less summarising).

12.	Remove all -capture program drop- lines in your ado-file. (See https://www.statalist.org/forums/forum/general-stata-discussion/general/1758652-on-capture-program-drop)

13.	Your adofiles uses a combination of temporary variables and hard-coded variables (the latter are mostly derived using your -egen- commands, I think – the stuff you refer to in your Calculations section in the text). Your Table 3 and discussion doesn’t make clear that the variable names for output are hard-coded, I think. Usually, creation of hard-coded variable names is deprecated by Stata programmers. Instead one would typically do calculations using tempvars and at the end create variables that are clones of the tempvars – and users could optionally choose what to call those variables. A signal advantage of this practice is that if the program stops prior to completion no permanent variables are left behind. Please reflect on what you’re doing in your program and, if you maintain your current practice, provide a persuasive justification for doing so explaining why it’s not a problem in your case.

14.	Why not add a -quietly compress- after creating your variables. My playing with your rain data suggests that space can be saved. [Addressing some of the issues mentioned in point #3 above might be mentioned here if it’s too hard to address them now?] 


All in all, in the light of the referee’s remarks and my reading, my decision is revise and resubmit. Please address the referee’s and my points.

When resubmitting, please submit a complete package of materials (code as well as article; this is to ensure we have everything in one place), plus a covering letter that sets out, point by point, how you have addressed each of the comments made by the referee and by me (general and specific). Remember to include log-files (and dta-files and graph files where relevant) to accompany "example" do-files - having them is essential for reproducibility and audit trails.

Before resubmitting, if you have not done so already, please ensure that you have read and followed the submission guidelines shown at https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/stj. Also look at some recently published SJ articles.

We would like to see any resubmission within the next 4 months. If that is likely to prove a problem, please get in touch and we’d be happy to discuss a later deadline.

Please ensure that all SJ-related correspondence is CC:ed to editors@stata-journal.com
